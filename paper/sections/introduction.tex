\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

% Structured as bullet points for later expansion.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Motivating question.} Can the Spectral Optimizer---a
    gradient-filtering wrapper around AdamW that eigendecomposes the
    $B \times B$ per-sample gradient similarity matrix and keeps only
    directions many samples agree on (Marchenko--Pastur-style
    threshold)---reduce the effect of noise when training on large, noisy
    financial time-series data? Concretely: does it improve out-of-sample
    predictive performance relative to tuned AdamW, and is any effect
    consistent across architectures?

  \item \textbf{Why the outcome was genuinely uncertain.} In prior
    (unpublished) work the same filter delivered strong label-noise
    robustness on MNIST at 90\% label corruption ($\sim$80\% accuracy vs.\
    $\sim$37\% for Adam), characterized as a ``coherence amplifier''. But
    the same work found it hurts when signal is weak and not yet
    gradient-dominant, and Feldman's long-tail memorization theory
    \citep{feldman2019longtailmemorization} predicts consensus filtering
    could suppress rare genuine signal in heavy-tailed financial data. Prior
    evidence pointed in both directions.

  \item \textbf{Design.} A pre-registered, fail-fast evaluation on the
    Numerai v5 tournament dataset (large, obfuscated, low-SNR financial
    features with era structure): era-purged tuning/verdict block
    separation with embargo, a frozen three-way verdict rule
    (helps / doesn't help / hurts) with a pre-registered
    practical-significance threshold, moving-block-bootstrap inference,
    compute-matched tuning budgets \citep{schneider2019deepobs}, mechanism
    controls, and engagement diagnostics on every run. Five experiments,
    all on single L40 GPUs, $\sim$15 GPU-minutes total.

  \item \textbf{Headline result (negative).} Under the pre-registered
    protocol the reportable claim is \emph{no evidence of benefit under the
    affordable tuning budget} (the spectral arm afforded $\sim$1
    compute-matched tuning trial vs.\ AdamW's 12). The raw numbers behind
    that downgraded wording: the filter significantly \emph{degraded}
    out-of-sample performance on both architectures (MLP: $-0.00527$ mean
    per-era \texttt{numerai\_corr}, 95\% CI $[-0.00886, -0.00181]$;
    GRU: $-0.00484$, CI $[-0.00758, -0.00211]$), erasing $\sim$82--83\% of
    a $+0.0064$ baseline edge.

  \item \textbf{Co-primary contribution 1: a target-independence proof.}
    For scalar-output MSE, the filter's engagement eigenspectrum is
    \emph{exactly} target-independent at fixed parameters: row
    normalization reduces each per-sample gradient to $\pm$(residual sign)
    $\times$ a target-independent Jacobian direction, so permuting targets
    conjugates the similarity matrix by an orthogonal sign matrix
    (verified in fp64 to $8.9\times10^{-16}$; independently re-verified at
    $2.1\times10^{-14}$ with scoping controls showing the theorem is
    non-vacuous). ``Engagement'' therefore cannot certify that the
    mechanism found target signal on this task class.

  \item \textbf{Co-primary contribution 2: matched mechanism controls.}
    MP-eigenselection is statistically indistinguishable from a norm- and
    $k$-matched \emph{random}-subspace projection on both architectures
    (MLP: $-0.00116$, CI $[-0.00366, +0.00134]$; GRU: $-0.00011$, CI
    $[-0.00233, +0.00215]$; equivalence at $\pm 0.004$ resolution). All
    three filtered arms (spectral, random-subspace, sign-agreement) hurt
    similarly: the harm tracks agreement-style update modification as a
    class, not the spectral machinery.

  \item \textbf{Robustness.} An independent results audit re-executed the
    load-bearing experiment on fresh seeds \{10, 11, 12\} and an
    independently constructed evaluation shard; the headline reproduced in
    category, direction, and magnitude, as did the spectral-vs-random
    null (6-seed pooled headline $-0.00537$, CI $[-0.00853, -0.00222]$).

  \item \textbf{Framing.} The contribution is the transfer verdict and its
    mechanistic explanation, not a new optimizer. A well-supported negative
    result was priced as a full deliverable from the outset; this paper
    reports one, with its scope limits stated plainly (subsampled,
    low-edge regime; single spectral operating point).
\end{itemize}
